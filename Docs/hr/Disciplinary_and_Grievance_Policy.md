## 1. Policy Overview & Objectives

NovaTech Solutions is committed to maintaining a fair, respectful, and productive workplace. This Disciplinary and Grievance Policy is designed to:

- Ensure that concerns about employee conduct or performance are addressed fairly and consistently
- Provide employees with a clear understanding of the standards of behavior expected of them
- Establish a formal process for employees to raise concerns, complaints, or grievances without fear of retaliation
- Protect the company's legitimate interests while also protecting employee rights
- Comply with all applicable federal, state, and local employment laws

This policy applies to all full-time and part-time employees of NovaTech Solutions. Contractors and consultants are subject to their contractual agreements and applicable law, not this policy.

Nothing in this policy creates an employment contract or alters the at-will nature of employment. NovaTech reserves the right to deviate from the steps outlined herein where circumstances warrant, particularly in cases of gross misconduct.

## 2. Disciplinary Policy

### 2.1 Purpose
The disciplinary policy aims to correct behavior and performance issues through a fair, progressive process, rather than to punish. Our goal is to give employees a genuine opportunity to improve before more serious action is taken.

### 2.2 Progressive Discipline Principle
NovaTech generally follows a progressive discipline model for most workplace misconduct:

1. **Verbal Warning**
2. **Written Warning**
3. **Final Written Warning**
4. **Termination of Employment**

However, the company reserves the right to begin at any step of the process — or to skip directly to termination — based on the severity of the misconduct, the employee's history, or the circumstances of the situation. In particular, **gross misconduct** may result in immediate termination without prior warnings.

## 3. Categories of Misconduct

### 3.1 Minor Misconduct
Minor misconduct typically involves a single incident or a pattern of low-severity behavior issues. Progressive discipline beginning at Step 1 (Verbal Warning) is the normal response.

**Examples of Minor Misconduct:**
- Occasional unexcused tardiness or early departures
- Failure to follow standard process (e.g., not logging time, skipping status updates)
- Minor breaches of workplace policy (e.g., dress code in a client setting)
- Excessive personal use of company resources (internet, phone)
- Careless work resulting in easily correctable errors
- Failure to complete required training within the extended grace period

### 3.2 Serious Misconduct
Serious misconduct is more significant and may warrant starting the process at Step 2 (Written Warning) or Step 3 (Final Written Warning), and in some cases, suspension pending investigation.

**Examples of Serious Misconduct:**
- Repeated instances of minor misconduct despite prior warnings
- Insubordination (deliberate refusal to follow reasonable instructions)
- Threatening or intimidating behavior (not rising to the level of physical violence)
- Unauthorized disclosure of confidential company information (non-deliberate, first offense)
- Harassment or discriminatory conduct (non-severe, single incident) — subject to separate Anti-Harassment investigation
- Falsification of expense reports or timesheets (minor amounts)
- Being under the influence of alcohol at work (first offense)
- Misuse of company data or systems (non-malicious)
- Significant attendance issues despite documented counseling

### 3.3 Gross Misconduct
Gross misconduct is severe enough that it fundamentally destroys the trust and confidence required for continued employment. Gross misconduct may result in immediate termination without prior warnings or notice (summary dismissal), subject to applicable law.

**Examples of Gross Misconduct:**
- Theft, fraud, or deliberate falsification of company records or financial data
- Physical violence, assault, or credible threats of violence toward any individual
- Sexual assault or serious sexual harassment
- Serious bullying, discrimination, or harassment based on protected characteristics
- Deliberate sabotage of company systems, products, or customer data
- Malicious disclosure or misuse of confidential company or customer information
- Possession, use, or distribution of illegal substances at work or company events
- Serious breach of NovaTech's Information Security Policy (e.g., intentional data exfiltration)
- Bribery, corruption, or serious conflicts of interest not disclosed as required
- Making false statements or providing false documentation during a company investigation
- Significant unauthorized access to systems or confidential records
- Conduct that brings the company into serious disrepute (e.g., illegal activity reported publicly)

This list is not exhaustive. Other conduct of comparable severity may also constitute gross misconduct at the company's discretion.

## 4. Disciplinary Process — Step by Step

### Step 1: Verbal Warning

**When Used:** First instance of minor misconduct, or early pattern concerns.

**Process:**
1. Manager identifies and documents the performance or conduct concern
2. Manager consults with People Ops HRBP before proceeding (mandatory)
3. Manager schedules a private meeting with the employee (may include People Ops representative)
4. Manager clearly explains: the specific issue, why it is a concern, what change is expected, and the timeline for improvement
5. Employee is given the opportunity to respond, ask questions, and share their perspective
6. Manager completes a **Verbal Warning Summary** form in Rippling/Lattice within 24 hours, documenting the discussion
7. A copy of the summary is placed in the employee's HR file. The employee signs to acknowledge receipt (signature does not imply agreement)

**Duration:** Verbal warnings remain active in the employee's file for **6 months**. After 6 months without further incident, the warning is considered expired for progressive discipline purposes (though it remains in the record).

### Step 2: Written Warning

**When Used:** Recurrence of misconduct after a Verbal Warning, or first instance of serious misconduct.

**Process:**
1. Manager consults with People Ops HRBP — People Ops must review the Written Warning document before delivery
2. People Ops drafts the Written Warning in collaboration with the manager
3. If the matter is complex, a brief investigation (interview with relevant parties, review of evidence) precedes the warning
4. A meeting is held with the employee, their manager, and a People Ops representative
5. The Written Warning letter is delivered and discussed. The letter includes:
   - Specific description of the misconduct with dates/examples
   - Reference to any prior Verbal Warning
   - Clear expected behavior going forward
   - Timeline for improvement (typically 30–90 days)
   - Consequences of further incidents (escalation to Final Written Warning or termination)
6. Employee signs the Written Warning to acknowledge receipt. Employee may submit a written response within 5 business days, which is attached to the file
7. Manager schedules regular check-ins during the improvement period (typically bi-weekly)

**Duration:** Written warnings remain active for **12 months**.

### Step 3: Final Written Warning

**When Used:** Recurrence of misconduct after Written Warning, or first instance of near-gross misconduct warranting this severity.

**Process:**
1. People Ops HRBP and the employee's VP must both approve proceeding to Final Written Warning
2. Legal review is required before any Final Written Warning is issued
3. A formal meeting is held: employee, manager, People Ops representative; employee may bring a support person (non-legal representative)
4. Final Written Warning letter includes:
   - Full summary of prior warnings and current incident
   - Non-negotiable improvement requirements
   - Timeline (typically 30–60 days)
   - Explicit statement that the next incident will result in termination
5. Employee is given the opportunity to respond formally in writing within 5 business days
6. The employee may be placed on administrative suspension (with pay) during this period in serious cases

**Duration:** Final Written Warnings remain active for **18 months**.

### Step 4: Termination

**When Used:** Failure to improve after Final Written Warning, or gross misconduct.

**Process:**
1. People Ops HRBP + Department VP + Legal must all align on the termination decision
2. A termination meeting is scheduled with the employee (manager + People Ops; may include Legal for complex cases)
3. Employee is informed of the decision, effective date, and separation terms
4. Employee receives a written termination letter
5. System access is revoked on the last day of employment (immediately upon meeting in gross misconduct cases)
6. COBRA and final paycheck information is provided per applicable law
7. People Ops completes separation documentation in Rippling within 48 hours

**Severance:** Severance eligibility is at NovaTech's discretion and is typically offered for non-performance terminations (e.g., role elimination, company restructuring), not for terminations for cause. Severance terms are documented in a Separation Agreement reviewed by Legal.

## 5. Summary Dismissal (Gross Misconduct)

In cases of gross misconduct, NovaTech may terminate employment immediately without following the progressive discipline steps.

**Summary Dismissal Process:**
1. Manager immediately reports the suspected gross misconduct to People Ops and Legal
2. If the situation warrants it (e.g., threat to safety, data breach), the employee may be placed on **paid administrative suspension** pending investigation (typically 3–5 business days)
3. People Ops conducts a prompt investigation (interviews, evidence review) — see §9 for investigation standards
4. Legal reviews findings and confirms the decision to terminate
5. If termination is confirmed: the meeting is held, termination letter is issued, access is revoked immediately
6. If investigation does not support termination: the employee is reinstated with back pay for any suspension period; appropriate alternative disciplinary action may still be taken

## 6. Appeals Process (Disciplinary)

Employees have the right to appeal any disciplinary decision (Written Warning, Final Written Warning, or Termination).

**How to Appeal:**
1. Employee submits a written appeal to People Operations at hrbp@novatechsolutions.com within **10 business days** of receiving the disciplinary decision
2. The appeal must state: the grounds for appeal (e.g., new evidence, procedural error, disproportionate sanction) and the outcome sought
3. The appeal is heard by a People Ops leader who was **not involved** in the original decision, typically at the VP or Director level
4. The appeal review includes: reviewing all documentation, meeting with the employee and relevant manager, and considering any new information presented
5. A written decision is provided within **15 business days** of the appeal being received
6. The appeal decision may: uphold the original decision, vary the sanction (increase or decrease), or overturn the decision entirely
7. The appeal decision is **final and binding**

Employees may bring a support person (a NovaTech colleague, not a lawyer) to any appeal meeting.

## 7. Grievance Policy

### 7.1 Purpose
The grievance policy provides a formal mechanism for employees to raise concerns about:
- Their treatment at work (including by managers or colleagues)
- Working conditions or terms of employment
- Perceived unfair treatment, bullying, or harassment
- Application of company policy
- Alleged breaches of employment law or contract

NovaTech treats all grievances seriously and investigates them promptly, impartially, and confidentially.

### 7.2 Principles
- Employees will not face retaliation for raising a grievance in good faith
- All parties involved in a grievance are entitled to be heard
- Grievances will be resolved as quickly as is practicable without sacrificing thoroughness
- NovaTech will make every effort to maintain confidentiality, sharing information only on a need-to-know basis

---

## 8. Grievance Process — Step by Step

### Step 1: Informal Resolution

Most workplace concerns can and should be resolved informally before a formal grievance is raised. Employees are encouraged to:
- **Speak directly** with the person whose behavior or action is the concern, if it is safe and appropriate to do so
- **Speak with their manager** about the concern (unless the manager is the subject of the concern)
- **Contact their People Ops HRBP** for coaching on how to address the issue informally

If the informal approach resolves the issue, no further action is required. If the concern is about the employee's direct manager, skip directly to Step 2.

### Step 2: Formal Grievance Submission

If informal resolution is not possible or appropriate, or if the informal approach has been tried and failed, the employee may submit a formal grievance.

**How to Submit:**
1. Complete the **NovaTech Formal Grievance Form** (available on the People Ops Notion page or by emailing hrbp@novatechsolutions.com)
2. Submit the completed form to hrbp@novatechsolutions.com or directly to the VP of People Operations
3. The form asks for:
   - Description of the grievance (as specifically as possible, with dates, individuals involved, and witnesses)
   - What outcome the employee is seeking
   - Any supporting documentation

**Acknowledgment:** People Ops will acknowledge receipt of the formal grievance within **2 business days**.

### Step 3: Initial Review & Investigator Assignment

Within **5 business days** of acknowledging the grievance:
- People Ops determines whether the grievance requires a formal investigation or can be resolved through facilitated discussion
- An investigator is assigned. The investigator is typically a senior People Ops professional or an external HR consultant, and must have no prior involvement in the matter
- If the grievance involves an allegation against an HR team member, an external investigator is appointed

## 9. Formal Grievance Investigation

### 9.1 Investigation Standards
NovaTech investigations are conducted according to the following standards:
- **Promptness:** Investigations are completed within **30 business days** where possible; complex matters may take up to 60 days
- **Impartiality:** The investigator has no personal interest in the outcome
- **Confidentiality:** Information shared during the investigation is kept as confidential as possible, consistent with completing a thorough investigation
- **Fairness:** Both the complainant and the respondent have the opportunity to present their perspective and any evidence

### 9.2 Investigation Steps
1. **Investigator briefing:** People Ops briefs the investigator on the complaint and available documentation
2. **Complainant interview:** Investigator meets with the employee who raised the grievance to understand the full picture
3. **Respondent interview:** Investigator meets with the individual(s) against whom the grievance is raised. They are informed of the general nature of the complaint (though not necessarily every detail) and given the opportunity to respond
4. **Witness interviews:** Investigator meets with any identified witnesses
5. **Document review:** Investigator reviews relevant emails, Slack messages, HR records, or other documentation (obtained through appropriate channels consistent with privacy law)
6. **Draft findings:** Investigator prepares a written findings report, including a summary of evidence, credibility assessments, and factual conclusions
7. **Decision:** People Ops (in consultation with Legal if needed) determines appropriate action based on findings

### 9.3 Possible Outcomes
- **No upheld findings:** The evidence does not support the grievance. Complainant is informed and advised of next steps if they disagree (appeal).
- **Partially upheld:** Some aspects of the grievance are supported by evidence. Appropriate remedial action is taken for those aspects.
- **Fully upheld:** The grievance is substantiated. Remedial action may include: formal apology, policy changes, changes to reporting structure, disciplinary action against the respondent (which follows the separate disciplinary process in §4)

---

## 10. Appeal Rights (Grievance)

Employees who are dissatisfied with the outcome of a formal grievance investigation may appeal within **10 business days** of receiving the outcome decision.

**Appeal Process:**
1. Submit a written appeal to the VP of People Operations or CEO (if the grievance involves the VP of People Operations)
2. Appeal grounds must include: why the employee believes the outcome is incorrect, what evidence was overlooked, or what procedural error occurred
3. The appeal is reviewed by a leader not previously involved in the investigation
4. Written outcome within **15 business days**
5. The appeal decision is final

## 11. Special Circumstances & Protections

### 11.1 Protection from Retaliation
Any employee who raises a grievance in good faith, participates as a witness in a grievance investigation, or reports suspected misconduct is protected from retaliation. Retaliation includes any adverse employment action — demotion, reduction in pay, schedule change, hostile treatment — that is connected to the protected activity.

Retaliation is itself a form of serious misconduct and may result in disciplinary action up to and including termination of the retaliating party.

### 11.2 Malicious Complaints
While employees are protected when raising concerns in good faith, making a deliberately false complaint with intent to harm another employee is itself misconduct. Evidence of malicious intent may result in disciplinary action.

### 11.3 Multiple Complaints
If multiple complaints are received about the same individual or situation, People Ops may conduct a combined investigation or handle them separately, depending on the facts.

### 11.4 Concurrent Legal Proceedings
If an employee or third party initiates legal proceedings related to the same matter, People Ops and Legal will coordinate on how to proceed with internal processes in a manner that does not prejudice either process.

## 12. Documentation Requirements

All disciplinary and grievance documentation must be maintained in Rippling HR records in accordance with the following retention schedule:

| Document Type | Retention Period |
|--------------|-----------------|
| Verbal Warning Summary | 3 years from date |
| Written Warning | 5 years from date |
| Final Written Warning | 7 years from date |
| Termination documentation | 7 years from date |
| Grievance investigation report | 7 years from date |
| Appeal decisions | 7 years from date |

All documentation is treated as confidential HR records and is accessible only to: the employee (their own file), their direct managers, People Ops team, Legal, and the executive team on a need-to-know basis.

## 13. Confidentiality

NovaTech is committed to handling all disciplinary and grievance matters as confidentially as possible. Individuals involved in a matter — as complainant, respondent, or witness — are asked to maintain confidentiality about the process and its details.

While complete confidentiality cannot be guaranteed (investigation requires sharing information with relevant parties), People Ops takes all reasonable steps to minimize the number of people who are aware of a grievance or disciplinary matter.

Breach of confidentiality by any party during a disciplinary or grievance process may itself constitute misconduct.
