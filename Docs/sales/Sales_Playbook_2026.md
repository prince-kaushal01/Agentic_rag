## 1. Introduction & Sales Strategy Overview

Welcome to the NovaTech Solutions 2026 Sales Playbook. This document is the definitive guide for how we win enterprise deals. Every AE, SDR, and Sales Manager should know this playbook inside and out.

**Our Go-to-Market Motion:**
NovaTech sells enterprise knowledge management and support automation software on an annual subscription (ARR) basis. Our primary motion is direct enterprise sales: high-touch, multi-stakeholder deals with average contract values of $75,000–$500,000+ per year.

Secondary motions:
- **Expansion:** Growing existing accounts through seat expansion, module upsells, and platform adoption
- **Channel:** Reseller and MSP partnerships (in pilot as of 2026; not the primary focus)

**2026 Sales Targets:**
- Total ARR target: $22.5M (up from $16.8M in 2025)
- New Logo ARR: $9.0M
- Expansion ARR: $5.5M
- Net Revenue Retention Target: 120%

Our 2026 sales team is structured in two tiers:
- **Enterprise AEs** (IC5): Named accounts, 500+ employee companies, average deal $150K–$500K ARR
- **Mid-Market AEs** (IC3): Target accounts 150–500 employees, average deal $50K–$150K ARR
- **SDRs/BDRs**: Outbound pipeline generation, booking meetings for AEs

## 2. Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)

Our Ideal Customer Profile represents the type of company most likely to buy, stay, and grow with NovaTech. Every prospecting and qualification activity should be measured against this profile.

### Primary ICP Characteristics

**Company Characteristics:**
- **Size:** 500–10,000 employees (sweet spot: 1,000–5,000)
- **Revenue:** $50M–$2B annually
- **Industry:** Technology (SaaS, IT services), Financial Services, Healthcare, Professional Services, Manufacturing
- **Geography:** North America (primary); UK/Western Europe (secondary, growing)
- **Business Model:** B2B organizations with large internal support or knowledge management needs
- **Software Maturity:** Currently using or has recently evaluated a competitor; not using spreadsheets/email as primary knowledge management tool (that's a discovery, not an ICP disqualifier, but affects deal timeline)

**Organizational Characteristics:**
- Has a dedicated IT, Customer Support, or Knowledge Management function
- Has experienced growth in headcount or product complexity in the past 24 months
- Faces regulatory or compliance requirements (GDPR, HIPAA, SOC 2, ISO 27001) that create documentation pressure
- Budget authority exists at the VP or Director level for software procurement

**Technology Stack Signals:**
- Uses Salesforce, HubSpot, or Zendesk (strong integration signal)
- Uses Confluence, SharePoint, or Notion (potential rip-and-replace opportunity or complement)
- Has a cloud-first or cloud-majority infrastructure (AWS, Azure, GCP)

**Negative ICP Signals (Likely to Disqualify):**
- Fewer than 200 employees (below minimum viability for our platform)
- No dedicated support, IT, or knowledge management function
- Government/public sector procurement (unless via approved channel partner)
- Budget < $30K/year for software of this category
- Currently under contract with a competitor for next 18+ months with high switching cost

## 3. Buyer Personas

NovaTech deals typically involve 3–6 stakeholders. Understanding each persona is critical for multi-threading effectively.

### Persona 1: The Economic Buyer — VP of IT / CIO

**Title Examples:** VP of Information Technology, Chief Information Officer, VP of IT Operations
**Role in Deal:** Final budget approval; risk assessment; strategic fit evaluation
**Company Size:** Usually at organizations 1,000+ employees

**Day-to-Day Concerns:**
- Consolidating the technology stack and reducing vendor sprawl
- Ensuring security, compliance, and data governance
- Managing IT support ticket volume and resolution time
- Supporting the business with scalable infrastructure

**Motivations for Buying NovaTech:**
- Reduce support ticket volume through self-service knowledge
- Improve IT helpdesk efficiency and MTTR (mean time to resolve)
- Meet compliance requirements for documented policies and procedures
- Eliminate homegrown or outdated knowledge systems

**How to Engage:**
- Focus on ROI, TCO, and enterprise-grade security/compliance
- Provide case studies from similar industries with measurable outcomes
- Arrange a security/architecture review early in the deal
- Never skip this person in your multi-threading strategy

**Key Questions for This Persona:**
- "What does your current IT support resolution time look like, and what's your target?"
- "How are you managing documentation and knowledge across a distributed team today?"
- "What security and compliance certifications does your vendor ecosystem need to meet?"

---

### Persona 2: The Champion — VP of Engineering / VP of Customer Support / Director of Knowledge Management

**Title Examples:** VP of Engineering, VP of Customer Experience, Director of Support Operations, Knowledge Manager
**Role in Deal:** Internal champion; day-to-day evaluator; drives the process from the inside

**Day-to-Day Concerns:**
- Reducing repetitive support questions (internally and externally)
- Onboarding new team members faster with better documentation
- Managing knowledge quality as the organization scales
- Reducing time engineers/support agents spend searching for answers

**Motivations for Buying NovaTech:**
- Make their team more efficient and less frustrated
- Improve new employee ramp time
- Get recognition for shipping a solution that the whole organization uses
- Demonstrate operational maturity

**How to Engage:**
- Deep product demo focusing on their team's specific workflows
- Get them hands-on with a trial or POC early
- Help them build the internal business case for the Economic Buyer
- Keep them close throughout the process — they are your internal seller

**Key Questions:**
- "Walk me through a typical week for one of your support agents. Where do they spend most of their time?"
- "When someone on your team can't find an answer, what do they do?"
- "What would it mean for your team if resolution time dropped by 30%?"

---

### Persona 3: The Technical Evaluator — IT Director / Solutions Architect / Security Engineer

**Title Examples:** IT Director, Head of IT Security, Principal Systems Architect
**Role in Deal:** Validates technical fit; raises (and must clear) security/integration blockers

**Day-to-Day Concerns:**
- Integration with existing systems (SSO, ticketing, CRM)
- Data residency and sovereignty requirements
- Uptime SLA and disaster recovery
- Implementation complexity and migration effort

**Motivations for Evaluation:**
- Ensure the solution doesn't create a security or compliance liability
- Validate that implementation won't require significant engineering resources
- Confirm the API and integration ecosystem fits their stack

**How to Engage:**
- Involve your Solutions Engineer (SE) early — this persona wants to talk to someone technical
- Prepare a Security Questionnaire response in advance (available from Customer Success)
- Provide architecture documentation and SOC 2 Type II report proactively
- Do a live integration walkthrough with Salesforce/Zendesk during the demo

## 4. Core Pain Points We Solve

### Pain Point 1: Knowledge Silos
Employees across large organizations can't find the information they need, leading to repeated questions, inconsistent answers, and lost productivity. NovaTech centralizes knowledge in a single, searchable, AI-powered platform.

**Quantified Impact:** Studies show knowledge workers spend ~30% of their time searching for information. NovaTech customers report 40–60% reduction in search time.

### Pain Point 2: Slow Support Resolution
Support teams spend excessive time answering repeat questions or escalating issues that should be self-service. NovaTech's AI-powered answer surfacing and deflection capability reduces ticket volume by 25–40%.

### Pain Point 3: Painful Employee Onboarding
New employees take too long to become productive because documentation is scattered, outdated, or inaccessible. NovaTech reduces average time-to-competency by 35%.

### Pain Point 4: Compliance Documentation Gaps
Organizations in regulated industries struggle to maintain audit-ready documentation. NovaTech provides version-controlled, role-permissioned documentation with audit trails.

### Pain Point 5: Scaling Knowledge at Hypergrowth
Fast-growing companies double headcount in 12–18 months and find their informal, person-to-person knowledge transfer model breaks down. NovaTech scales knowledge infrastructure ahead of headcount.

## 6. Discovery Call Framework (MEDDIC)

All discovery calls at NovaTech are run using the MEDDIC framework. Every field must be populated in Salesforce before the deal advances past the Discovery stage.

### M — Metrics (Quantify the Pain)
- What are the specific, measurable outcomes the buyer needs?
- "What does your current resolution time look like, and what's your target?"
- "How many support tickets does your team handle per week? What % are repeat questions?"
- "How long does it take a new hire to reach full productivity?"
- "What's the cost of one hour of your senior engineer's time? How many of those hours go to answering repeated questions?"

### E — Economic Buyer (Who Writes the Check?)
- Who in your organization is responsible for approving a purchase of this size?
- "What does the budget approval process look like for software in this price range?"
- "Have you already allocated budget for this type of solution?"
- Do not proceed to Demo stage without a confirmed Economic Buyer name in Salesforce.

### D — Decision Criteria (How Will They Decide?)
- "What criteria will you use to evaluate solutions?"
- "Is there a formal RFP or scoring rubric?"
- "What does success look like for you in the first 90 days?"
- "Beyond features, what factors matter most — vendor stability, implementation support, pricing flexibility?"

### D — Decision Process (How Do Decisions Get Made?)
- "Walk me through how you typically evaluate and buy software at this price point."
- "Who are the key stakeholders in the decision, and what role does each play?"
- "Do you have existing vendor relationships or preferences that apply here?"
- "What's your target go-live date, and what's driving that timeline?"

### I — Identify Pain (Find the Compelling Event)
- "What's driving you to look at solutions now, rather than 6 months ago?"
- "What happens if this problem isn't solved by [target date]?"
- "How is this issue affecting your team's ability to deliver on their goals?"
- The "compelling event" is what creates urgency. Always identify it.

### C — Champion (Find and Develop Your Internal Seller)
- "Who else in your organization has a stake in solving this problem?"
- "Are you the person who would use NovaTech day to day, or are there others?"
- "Would you be comfortable making an introduction to [Economic Buyer] once we've had a chance to show you the platform?"
- Your Champion needs to understand the value, believe in it, and be willing to sell it internally.

## 7. Qualification Criteria

Use BANT+ as a quick pre-qualification overlay before committing significant time:

| Criterion | Questions | Disqualify If... |
|-----------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Budget** | Is there an allocated budget or can they create one? | No budget path and deal > $60K ARR |
| **Authority** | Can you access the Economic Buyer? | Champion refuses to introduce EB after 3 attempts |
| **Need** | Is the pain genuine and quantifiable? | No clear pain articulated |

| **Competition** | Are they evaluating alternatives? | Sole-sourced by a competitor at exec level |

## 8. Objection Handling Scripts

### Objection 1: "Your pricing is too high / We can get this cheaper elsewhere."

**Response:** "I hear you — and pricing is always an important factor. Can I ask a couple of questions? What specifically feels misaligned: the per-user rate, the total contract value, or the ROI you expect to see? [Listen] Most of our customers find that when they calculate the cost of the problem — lost productivity, elevated ticket volume, slow onboarding — the investment pays back in under 12 months. Would it be helpful if we put together a business case together that speaks to your specific numbers?"

**Backing Data:** Average NovaTech customer ROI payback period: 9.2 months. Reference: GlobalTech Inc case study.

---

### Objection 2: "We already have Confluence / SharePoint — why do we need this?"

**Response:** "That's one of the most common situations we see. The question isn't whether those tools can store documents — they absolutely can. The question is whether they're actually working the way you need them to. When your support agent gets a complex ticket at 4pm on a Friday, how quickly can they find the right answer in Confluence? [Pause] Our customers tell us the issue isn't that they don't have a tool — it's that the information isn't findable or current when it matters most. NovaTech layers AI-powered search, smart suggestions, and automated freshness checks on top of or alongside what you have."

---

### Objection 3: "We're not ready to prioritize this right now — maybe in Q3."

**Response:** "Totally fair — a lot of our conversations start that way. Can I ask what's driving the Q3 timing? [Listen] The reason I ask is that implementation typically takes 6–8 weeks, and onboarding your team fully takes another 4–6 weeks. If Q3 is when you need this running, we'd need to start in April to hit that. Is there any reason we couldn't start the evaluation now so you're not rushed when you're ready to move? We have customers who ran a POC while finalizing budget — it gives you real data to justify the investment internally."

---

### Objection 4: "We had a bad experience with a knowledge management vendor before."

**Response:** "I really appreciate you sharing that — and I want to make sure we don't repeat that experience. Would you tell me a bit more about what went wrong? [Listen carefully] Those are exactly the kinds of issues we've designed NovaTech to solve: [address specific pain points mentioned]. We'd also love to get you connected with a reference customer who was in a similar situation — someone who came to us after a previous vendor disappointment. Would that be useful?"

---

### Objection 5: "Your security/compliance isn't sufficient for our requirements."

**Response:** "Security is absolutely non-negotiable, and we take it seriously. Can you tell me specifically what requirements you have? [Listen] Here's what I'd suggest: let me connect you with our Solutions Engineer and we'll walk through our SOC 2 Type II report, our data residency options, and our SSO and permission architecture in a dedicated security review. We do this regularly for regulated industries. Would 45 minutes with our SE and your IT security lead this week work?"

## 9. Competitive Differentiation

### vs. Accenture AI Suite
- **Their Strength:** Established brand, massive services partner ecosystem, strong in Fortune 100
- **Their Weakness:** Extremely expensive (3–5x NovaTech pricing), long implementation (6–18 months), requires Accenture professional services
- **Our Winning Message:** "You get enterprise-grade results without the enterprise-sized implementation headache. NovaTech deploys in 6–8 weeks, not 6–8 months."

### vs. DataBridge Pro
- **Their Strength:** Strong analytics and reporting; popular in BI teams
- **Their Weakness:** Not designed for knowledge management; weak AI search; poor support integrations
- **Our Winning Message:** "DataBridge is excellent for data warehousing. Our platform is built from the ground up for how your teams actually create, find, and use knowledge every day."

### vs. FlowLogic
- **Their Strength:** Good UI, strong SMB and mid-market presence, lower price point
- **Their Weakness:** Doesn't scale above 500 users well; limited enterprise security features; no SSO for most editions; poor customer support for enterprise customers
- **Our Winning Message:** "FlowLogic is a great tool for smaller teams. For an organization your size, you'll quickly outgrow it — and the migration costs are painful. NovaTech is built for scale from day one."

## 10. Deal Stages & Exit Criteria

All opportunities in Salesforce must be at the correct stage with documented exit criteria met before advancing.

| Stage | Description | Exit Criteria to Advance |
|-------|-------------|------------------------|
| **1 — Prospecting** | Lead identified; no contact made | Meeting booked with qualified contact |
| **2 — Discovery** | Initial call completed | MEDDIC fields > 70% complete; pain confirmed |
| **3 — Demo/Evaluation** | Demo completed; POC in progress (if applicable) | Demo done; evaluation criteria confirmed in writing |
| **4 — Proposal** | Formal proposal/pricing delivered | Proposal received and acknowledged; EB confirmed |
| **5 — Negotiation** | Legal and commercial terms in discussion | Verbal agreement on price and terms |
| **6 — Closed Won** | Contract signed; revenue recognized | Countersigned MSA/Order Form received |
| **7 — Closed Lost** | Deal lost | Lost reason documented; competitor or reason identified |

Close date must be updated weekly. Opportunities with no activity for 14+ days are flagged in Salesforce and escalated in the weekly pipeline review.
